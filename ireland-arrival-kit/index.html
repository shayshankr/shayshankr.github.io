The Ireland Arrival Kit

29 apps for moving to Ireland, sorted by what they're actually for. Nine are apps most checklists miss — two for the visa wait itself, the rest for housing, government registration and moving money — each marked new below with the reason it matters.

Before you download: a lot of these — the banks, the supermarkets, MyGovID, the health insurers — only show up on the Irish Play Store or App Store. Search for them from an Indian account and you'll often get nothing, or "not available in your country."

Two ways round it: change your Play Store / App Store country to Ireland in your account settings (Google allows this once a year; Apple lets you browse and grab free apps from another country's store without needing a local card), or just wait and set them up once you've actually landed with an Irish address.

Irish Visa Tracker isn't affected — it's published for applicants who are still in India, so you can download and use it right now, wherever you are.

Before you land

2 apps
Irish Visa Trackernew
Enter your submission date, VAC city and visa type, and it estimates your decision window, flags a passport or document about to expire, and pushes a notification if your case is running late enough to email the embassy about. Built for the exact question every applicant asks: "why is it taking so long, and when should I actually worry."
Irish Visa Decision Checkernew
A browser extension that checks the embassy's published decision list for you, instead of you opening the page and searching your own name every morning. (Pending Chrome Web Store review — link goes live once approved.)

Getting around

8 apps
LUAS
Dublin's tram network. Buy tickets in the app and see when the next tram actually arrives, so you're not guessing at the stop.
TFI
The umbrella app for Transport for Ireland. Plan a journey by bus, train or tram anywhere in the country and buy the ticket in one place.
TFI Live
Shows real-time arrival times for buses and trains. Open this one when all you want to know is "how many minutes."
Moovit
Compares walking, bus and tram options for the same trip and picks the fastest. Good backup when TFI is slow to load.
Leap Top-Up
Tops up your Leap Card, the physical card most people tap on and off buses and trams with. Cheaper than paying cash on board.
FreeNow
Ireland's taxi app. Uber barely operates here — FreeNow is what locals actually open when they need a cab.
GoCar
Hourly car rental parked around the city. Handy for an IKEA run or a day trip without owning a car.
Irish Railnew
For intercity trains — Dublin to Cork, Galway, Belfast. TFI handles local transport well, but this is where the actual intercity timetables and tickets live.

Money & banking

3 apps
AIB
One of Ireland's two biggest banks. You'll need an Irish current account for salary, rent and direct debits.
Revolut
A digital account you can usually open before you even land. Decent exchange rates, and somewhere to hold money while your Irish bank account is still being set up.
Wisenew
The cheapest way to move money between India and Ireland. Uses the real exchange rate and shows the fee upfront, unlike most bank transfers.

Home & shopping

9 apps
Aldi
One of the three supermarkets most people shop at day to day — strong on price.
Tesco
The widest reach of the big three, and its Clubcard app gives regular discounts on a weekly shop.
Lidl Plus
Coupons and discounts tied to your account, refreshed weekly. Worth activating on day one, not just downloading.
IKEA
Furnishing a flat from empty is expensive anywhere. The app has stock checks and delivery slots, so you're not hauling flat-pack boxes home on the LUAS.
Decathlon
Affordable sports and outdoor gear — rain jackets and warm layers matter more here than back home.
Boots
Ireland's main pharmacy chain. The app tracks your Advantage Card points and lets you order repeat prescriptions ahead of time.
Caffè Nero
Coffee loyalty stamps. Small thing, adds up to a free coffee every few weeks.
Daft.ienew
The one you can't skip. Almost every room, apartment and house for rent in Ireland is listed here first. Set up alerts before you land — good places go within hours of posting.
SuperValunew
The fourth big supermarket, strong in areas Tesco and Aldi don't cover as well. Its Real Rewards scheme gives cash back on shopping.

Health & government

3 apps
MyGovIDnew
How you register for a PPS number, which you need to get paid, open some accounts, and use public services. One of the first things to sort after arrival.
VHI or Layanew
Whichever private health insurer your visa route requires. The app is where you book appointments, submit claims, and pull up proof of cover if asked.
An Post
Tracks parcels and post, and also runs An Post Money — a lighter-weight banking option if you want a second one alongside AIB.

Staying connected

4 apps
Dublin Airport
Flight status, live security queue times, and parking. Still useful long after you've landed, for whenever family visits.
Virgin Plus
Manage your SIM plan, check remaining data, and top up without hunting for a shop.
Eventbrite
A fast way to find meetups and community events — one of the easier ways to make friends in a new city.
Met Éireannnew
Ireland's official weather service. Generic weather apps get Irish weather wrong often enough that locals use this one specifically — it also issues real storm warnings.
